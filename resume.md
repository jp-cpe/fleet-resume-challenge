# Putting Fleet on your resume

Once you've worked through the challenge and published your blog post, the final step is
translating that hands-on experience into something hiring managers can see. This guide walks
through how to present the Fleet Resume Challenge on your resume, LinkedIn, and in interviews.

> 🏗️  Keep your hard hat on, this content is under construction.

## Why it belongs on your resume

The challenge gives you real, demonstrable device management experience: a Fleet server you
deployed end-to-end, hosts enrolled across multiple platforms, and configuration managed as code
with GitOps. That's exactly the kind of practical, provable work that sets you apart from
candidates with only theoretical knowledge.

## What to highlight

- **Deployment:** You stood up and own a live Fleet server end-to-end.
- **Cross-platform enrollment:** Hosts enrolled across macOS, Windows, Linux, iOS, and Android.
- **GitOps:** Managing devices as code and using CI/CD workflows to make changes.
- **osquery:** Querying and reporting on fleet-wide device state.
- **MDM:** Managing devices, including Apple MDM, certificates, and setup experience.

## How to phrase it

Frame each item around outcomes and tools, not just tasks. For example:

> Deployed and operated a self-hosted Fleet device management platform, enrolling hosts across
> macOS, Windows, and Linux and managing policies, software, and MDM configuration as code with
> GitOps.

## Proof to link

- Your public GitHub repo with GitOps configs, queries, and policies
- Screenshots of your Fleet instance
- The [blog post](modules/16-blog-post.md) describing what you learned
- The **Fleet Certified GitOps Administrator – Level 1** certification, if you earned it

## Roles this maps to

- Client Platform Engineer
- Device Management / Endpoint Engineer
- IT roles that manage fleets of devices
